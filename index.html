01 / DS @ Meta

What I worked on, and what working on it taught me.

The role is being redrawn, not eliminated.

I’m a genuine AI proponent. I use AI-native workflows every day, and I think they’re transformative for how analysis gets done. AI handles the parts of the job that used to take the most time: pulling the data, writing the query, scaffolding the chart.

What AI still doesn’t do well is the part that actually matters. Knowing what question to ask. What a finding means for the business. Which trade-offs are worth making. Models still hallucinate. They lack the contextual framing to recognize when a metric is measuring the wrong thing. They’ll happily produce a confident answer to a question that shouldn’t have been asked in the first place.

So the most valuable thing I bring isn’t query-writing speed (AI handles that). It’s the framing. The pushback. The willingness to say this metric doesn’t capture what we actually care about, and then go define one that does. That’s the part I find most interesting, and increasingly, where I think the role is heading.

At Meta Reality Labs, I worked on Meta AI glasses: first on Assistive Tech (features for users with disabilities), then on Capture Experiences (photo and video capture on the glasses).

01

Shipped A/B experiments

Drove data-backed product decisions on the Meta AI glasses capture stack, used by millions of users worldwide. Led the experiment analysis behind the 60FPS video rollout across all device generations. On a capture power throttling test, identified that the stat-sig lift was concentrated on older device generations and recommended device-specific policies to unlock additional engagement on newer ones. On Hyperlapse and Slow-Motion via voice commands, partnered with cross-functional teams to ship as the right business call despite a neutral experiment outcome.

Experimentation · A/B testing at scale

02

Causal inference for strategy

Authored the Capture Engagement Lever Analysis to investigate why weekly capture engagement was flat despite strong WAU growth. Decomposed engagement by demographics, weekday vs. weekend usage, non-capture feature usage, and downstream retention; applied causal inference to confirm a relationship between non-capture feature usage and capture engagement. Recommended shifting team goaling from adoption to depth-of-engagement metrics. The analysis directly shaped 2026 H2 strategy and roadmap.

Causal inference · lever decomposition

03

AI-native workflows in practice

Leveraged internal AI agents integrated with Claude, Gemini, and ChatGPT to deliver 3 simultaneous experiment deep-dives in under 2 weeks, including query generation, validation, and rapid onboarding to new systems. The leverage isn’t in letting AI do the analysis. It’s in letting AI handle the mechanical parts so the human work (framing, judgment, pushback) gets more of the day.

AI-native workflows

04

Defining what “success” meant

Redefined the canonical end-to-end capture success metric, replacing an approach that systematically misreported success (in the process of being adopted as the team standard for capture reporting). On Assistive Tech, reframed success measurement to device-level retention, which became the new team strategy and surfaced a 3-feature combination within the Blind and Low Vision community that drove disproportionately high retention.

Metric definition · product framing

05

Applied AI on real user behavior

Authored the first AI-powered content classification study on Meta AI glasses photo captures: 714K images across 34 content categories. Found that most users capture only one type of content, while users capturing multiple content types are meaningfully more weekly-active, validated against control groups with similar device usage patterns.

Applied AI · behavioral analysis

06

Diagnosed a firmware regression

Identified and root-caused a firmware-rollout regression that caused a sharp spike in capture errors across all device types. Established device-coverage requirements that prior analyses had missed for a substantial share of affected users. The kind of cross-stack work that requires both data instinct and a model of how the system actually fits together.

Production debugging · cross-stack

AI is the multiplier. The data scientist is the one deciding what to multiply, what the result actually means, and what to do about it. That’s the version of the role I’m building toward.
